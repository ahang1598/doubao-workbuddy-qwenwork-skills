# Visual Directions

Establish a direction before authoring. A direction is a **composition grammar
+ domain grammar**, not a palette preset. Each entry proposes five axes so the
deck stays coherent across slides; its `Forbid` list names common generic
defaults to challenge, not a closed list of prohibited creative choices.

Prefer one of the four below when it genuinely fits. They are proven starting
points, not a catalog ceiling. Adapt one or invent a different direction when
that better serves the brief. Load `frontend-design` when available for an
unusual or hybrid aesthetic; otherwise reason directly against the same five
axes plus a `forbid:` list of at least three generic defaults. Write the axes
down before authoring so the idea is more than a palette. Override any default
that would reduce clarity, accessibility, factual accuracy, task completion,
or the final artifact's effectiveness, and carry that intentional exception
consistently.

When *editing* a template, do not pick a direction: sample the
template's palette + fonts and honor them (see `editing.md § Step 0`).
Directions are for from-scratch decks.

## 1. Institutional restraint

**Best for**: investor decks, QBR, earnings, financial reviews,
strategy, board / compliance / SOE / government reports, research /
academic summaries. Default fallback for a generic corporate brief.

**Palette family**: deep navy or graphite primary; ivory or warm-white
bg; one gold or soft-red accent reserved for delta / risk / decision
signaling. Never use accent as a decorative fill.

**Image language**: real, verified photography — offices, facilities,
skylines at dusk, portraits with even light. No stock people-around-a-
laptop, no AI-generated concepts unless labeled illustrative.

**Cover silhouette**: two-panel split. Dark panel carries title +
subtitle + eyebrow; light panel carries a single hero photo, no
decoration. One hairline rule separates the panels.

**Content silhouettes**: KPI strip (4 large numbers), 2- or 3-column
comparison, evidence table with a status column, timeline with
milestone cards, one closing statement page. Every data page carries
a one-sentence management judgment.

**Type**: conservative serif or high-x-height sans title (~36–44pt);
sans body (~14–16pt); numbers set in the header face at scale.
Hierarchy from weight + size, not color.

**Motif**: thin gold or accent hairline reappearing under section
titles and around hero photos. Square corners everywhere.

**Forbid**: rounded cards, shadows, gradients, decorative icons on
every card, purple/blue neon accents, three identical card rows on
every content page, "conclusion" as the deck's only page type.

### Leader-reporting overlay (gov / SOE / internal review briefs)

When the brief is a leader-briefing — 汇报 / 督查 / 经营分析 / 风险处置 /
工作总结 — three additional rules apply:

- **Ask once, then commit**: 简版 (6–10 页，结论先行) or 详细材料版 (10–18 页，
  问题 + 依据 + 责任 + 节点)? Default to 详细版 when the user says "直接做" or
  the material is obviously complex.
- **Visible density floor**: 简版 content pages carry 100–220 visible
  CJK characters (or equivalent data / structure). 详细版 pages carry
  180–360; complex problem pages up to ~420 in 2–4 blocks. Do not
  hide the decision in speaker notes; do not shrink type below ~16pt
  to fit.
- **Every non-cover / non-divider page** carries at least two of
  {conclusion, evidence, next-step}. Deploy pages must show owner +
  deadline. Titles are judgment or action sentences ("三季度完成率达
  92%，跨部门遗留事项收口"), not topic labels ("相关情况" / "工作介绍").
  Red is for risk / decision prompts only, never for section headers.

**Narrative skeleton** (choose per material, not mechanical):
封面 → 核心结论 → 背景与任务来源 → 现状与成效 → 主要问题 → 原因与风险
→ 重点措施 → 责任与节点 → 需协调 / 需决策事项 → 下一步安排.

**Delivery gate**: page by page, can the leader form a judgment,
find the supporting fact, know the next step, see who's responsible,
and identify what they need to decide? A page that reads as "one
title + three slogans" gets rewritten, not polished.

## 2. Editorial story

**Best for**: city / travel / culture intros, brand or restaurant
long-reads, feature stories, retrospectives, magazine-style essays,
launches with strong photography.

**Palette family**: one warm neutral (cream / bone / paper) + one
saturated brand color drawn from the subject (coral, rust, ochre,
deep green). Ink-black type. No cool corporate blue unless the
subject specifically calls for it.

**Image language**: candid editorial photography, tactile materials,
natural light, restrained saturation. Big single photos over
collages. Detail crops (a hand, a plate, a doorway) beat wide
establishing shots.

**Cover silhouette**: full-bleed photo with a corner-anchored title
block, or an offset title over a paper-textured field. Never a
centered title on a plain color.

**Content silhouettes**: statement page (one sentence at 40–72pt),
image-right or image-left splits with running body, a wide-band
photo page, a data-hero page with one number carrying the whole
slide, a closing photo instead of a wordmark.

**Type**: expressive serif or high-contrast display headline; quiet
sans body; generous leading; asymmetric alignment. Kicker / eyebrow
text small-caps, tracked +80 to +140.

**Motif**: thin rule + a small folio number; one recurring editorial
device (a caption band, a marginalia column, a page-corner index).

**Forbid**: KPI dashboards, dense card grids, three-card rows on
every page, generic gradients, tiny photos crammed inside cards,
"business blue" applied to a culture brief.

## 3. Culture texture

**Best for**: 古诗词, 节气, 国学, 国画, 国风, 水墨, heritage, literature,
food stories, calligraphy, cultural programs, museum companion decks.

**Palette family**: rice-paper bg (`#F1EBDD` range), ink-black type,
one seal-red or one 青绿 accent used at most once per slide. Never
more than three colors on a page.

**Image language**: material close-ups (paper, silk, wood, water),
atmospheric place photography, restrained illustration, ink-wash
paintings when authentic. No AI-generated "Chinese aesthetic"
composites — they read wrong to native readers.

**Cover silhouette**: full-bleed ink wash or paper texture with a
vertically-set title (single column, right side or centered), 印章
seal offset to one corner. Or a spare left-third title + right two-
thirds atmospheric photo.

**Content silhouettes**: vertical poem column (54–120pt lead
character), horizontal narrative axis with 3–6 dated nodes, quote
band over paper texture, three-panel image montage, one closing
seal page.

**Type**: 宋体 or 楷体 (Songti / Kaiti) for headlines; a quiet Hei
face for body. Latin fallback in a companion serif. Wide tracking on
eyebrow labels is fine here — this is a culture where breathing type
is native.

**Motif**: paper texture, seal red mark, thin ink rule, occasional
vertical text run. The motif carries across pages; do not switch
textures mid-deck.

**Forbid**: corporate KPI dashboards, white plastic cards, gradient
fills, cool blue palettes, generated stock photography of "Chinese
elements", pixel-perfect gridded matrices that read as PowerPoint.

## 4. Signal system

**Best for**: AI / software / infra / product launches, technical
strategy, operating-model decks, engineering roadmaps, DevRel and
platform stories.

**Palette family**: near-black or graphite bg; one high-chroma
accent (cyan, lime, magenta, or amber) used sparingly on data and
titles; hard whites and mid grays for supporting text. Never all-
purple, never neon on every card.

**Image language**: precise cinematic environments (data centers,
lab shots, controlled interiors), abstract geometric renders, minimal
iconography, hard negative space. Real screenshots beat concept
mocks.

**Cover silhouette**: full-bleed dark with oversized sans title, one
accent line marking the horizon of the type; or a code / diagram
motif offset behind the title. Never a hero product on a white
gradient.

**Content silhouettes**: oversized-headline statement page, dark
data-hero, chart page with visible axis grid, architecture layer
diagram (native shapes, not iconography), image-left system detail,
closing wordmark page.

**Type**: oversized geometric sans (~64–96pt titles); mono or
condensed sans for labels and numbers; terse copy, no long
paragraphs. High contrast between title weight and body.

**Motif**: one recurring geometric element (a corner tick, a hairline
grid, a signal contour). Accent color appears once per page, never
twice.

**Forbid**: generic purple glow, repeated three-card rows, soft
lifestyle photography, "friendly" doodled icons, pastel palettes,
white plastic cards under a dark title.

## Cross-direction rules

Apply to every deck regardless of direction.

### Fingerprint the deck up front

Once you pick a direction, freeze these five axes in a comment at
the top of your build script:

- **palette** — the 6 hex slots you'll pass to `helpers.Palette`
- **image_style** — one string appended to every `image_prompt`
- **cover silhouette** — the one composition the cover will use
- **content silhouettes** — the compositions the current material suggests
- **motif** — the recurring device that carries across pages

Treat the fingerprint as a working hypothesis. Override it when another
composition materially improves clarity, then keep the intentional change
coherent with the rest of the deck.

### Cross-slide coherence

Let the information architecture decide when compositions repeat or vary.
`deck_style.py --rhythm` reports limited OOXML evidence as advisory `info`;
never add media, components, or variation merely to clear it.

### Multiple versions differ on all five axes

If asked for "another version," vary all five axes when useful: palette,
image language, cover silhouette, type hierarchy, and motif. A recolor alone
usually is not a meaningfully different version.

### Image_style suffix pattern

Declare the direction's image_style once, append everywhere:

```python
image_style = (
    "corporate architecture, blue glass facade, dusk light, cool tones, "
    "no text, no logos"
)
prompt = f"Shanghai pudong skyline at dusk — {image_style}"
```

### Wiring

Build a `helpers.Style` from the direction's palette + fonts and
hand it to every component call. See the wiring block in
`from_scratch.md`.
