# Universal Poster Router

Use this reference when the input category is unfamiliar, when several styles appear plausible, or when a casual snapshot has no campaign brief. Route by buying mechanism before aesthetics.

## 1. Extract four product signals

Record one answer for each axis:

| Axis | Options | What it controls |
|---|---|---|
| Form | rigid / soft / transparent / articulated / multi-part | truth and negative-geometry locks |
| Material | matte / glossy / metallic / textile / glass-liquid / food-organic | lighting and scene surfaces |
| Use | worn / held / consumed / displayed / installed / operated | interaction proof |
| Desire | performance / comfort / taste / beauty / status / convenience / price / identity / surprise | buying mechanism |

If `desire` is unclear, infer it from `use` and material. Do not infer a factual product claim.

## 2. Select the buying mechanism

Choose exactly one primary mechanism and at most one secondary mechanism.

| Mechanism | Reader question | Required proof | Best layout grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch authority | Why is this important? | monumental product + disciplined feature rail | centered or asymmetric monument |
| Functional proof | Why is it better? | visible construction/use + one dominant proof and up to two support points | headline → proof → hero → CTA or slogan close |
| Routine convenience | How does it fit my life? | pack/product + use action + result | product-result-routine triangle |
| Sensory desire | What will it feel/taste like? | material macro + interaction effect | refreshment tower or soft exhibit |
| Cultural value | Why does it feel meaningful? | craft/material + cultural scene | calm centered exhibit |
| Urgent conversion | Why buy now? | verified offer + SKU/price mapping | campaign stack or promotion arena |
| Social identity | Why share or identify with it? | audience tension + narrative scene | collage or narrative stage |
| Surprise/collectability | What might I discover? | reveal action + product/IP hierarchy | controlled burst |

Never select urgent conversion when no verified offer exists. Never select functional proof when the feature cannot be seen or supplied.

## 3. Set information density

Density is a commercial decision, not a decoration preference.

### D1 — Editorial premium: 6–8 modules

Use for status, beauty, cultural value, craftsmanship, fragrance, jewelry, gifts, and premium single products.

Required modules:

1. brand/eyebrow;
2. headline;
3. support line;
4. one coordinated proof rail;
5. hero product;
6. badge or material macro;
7. CTA for conversion or closing slogan for publicity;
8. optional trust footer.

Richness target: four visual layers, only one card family, abundant calm contrast.

### D2 — Commerce standard: 8–11 modules

Use for food, beverage, appliances, electronics, household goods, wearables, footwear, and daily-use products.

Required modules:

1. brand;
2. headline;
3. support;
4. benefit rail;
5. hero product;
6. use/result proof;
7. badge;
8. CTA for conversion, or non-interactive slogan for publicity;
9. trust or brand closure;
10–11. optional verified offer or seasonal cue.

Richness target: four or five visual layers, two coordinated container sizes, one repeated motif.

### D3 — Campaign dense: 11–15 modules

Use only for verified promotions, multi-SKU retail, IP reveals, or social narratives.

Group every module into:

1. hook super-container;
2. hero/story super-container;
3. conversion super-container.

Richness target: five layers, two or three container families, one calm contour around the hero. Reject any fourth equal attention anchor.

## 4. Choose the six-band blueprint

All numbers are percentages of poster height and may overlap by 2–6%.

| Blueprint | A Brand | B Promise | C Proof | D Hero world | E Action | F Closure |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Centered monument | 0–7 | 7–25 | 24–35 | 34–78 | 79–91 | 91–100 |
| Asymmetric monument | 0–8 | 8–30 | 28–45 | 25–80 | 80–92 | 92–100 |
| Product-result triangle | 0–7 | 7–25 | 24–35 | 32–78 | 77–91 | 91–100 |
| Diagonal sport/editorial | 0–6 | 5–26 | 24–35 | 30–78 | 78–92 | 92–100 |
| Refreshment tower | 0–7 | 7–25 | 24–42 | 32–80 | 78–92 | 92–100 |
| Campaign stack | 0–8 | 7–30 | 27–40 | 32–72 | 70–91 | 91–100 |
| Social narrative | 0–8 | 7–28 | 22–48 | 28–83 | 80–94 | 94–100 |

## 5. Set scale and overlap

- Headline: 12–22% height; dense campaigns may use 22–28%.
- Single hero product: 45–70% width or 35–62% height.
- Multi-SKU anchor: 30–45% width; supports 18–30%; accessories 10–18%.
- Benefit rail: 7–12% height.
- CTA/action dock for conversion, or slogan/brand dock for publicity: 8–13% height.
- Trust closure: 5–9% height.
- Keep 3–6% calm contour around the most important product edge.
- Let exactly one hero element cross a band boundary.

## 6. Build visual richness without fake information

Assign one function to every layer:

| Layer | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Foreground | depth/frame | cropped fabric, glass edge, leaf, platform rim |
| Product | sellable truth | one hero or explicit SKU set |
| Interaction | use/sensation | airflow, pour, splash, hand, motion path |
| Environment | context/scale | room, street, store, pavilion, city |
| Information | decision support | headline, rail, badge, conditional CTA, slogan, trust |

At least three layers are mandatory. Standard posters target four; dense campaign posters target five. Remove any object that does not prove use, direct attention, show scale, reinforce material, or close conversion.

Richness also needs specificity. Before approving a scene, name two devices that could not be transferred unchanged to an unrelated product. Examples: a cap-profile thermal arc for a bottle, a hem-shaped shadow sweep for a dress, an outsole-derived lane pattern for a shoe, or a grille-derived acoustic wavefront for a speaker. If the devices could be pasted onto any product, the direction is still generic.

## 7. Resolve unfamiliar categories

Use product signals rather than forcing a category match:

- jewelry/watch: status + worn → D1, soft premium or quiet luxury, centered/asymmetric exhibit;
- bag/apparel: identity + worn → D2, editorial lifestyle or social narrative;
- dress/apparel without model: identity + textile → D1 or D2, asymmetric fashion editorial with plausible support, material macro, and hem/print movement;
- vacuum bottle/thermos: convenience + held → D2, material-proof still life or a clearly divided hot/cold concept; never mix steam and ice on one undivided surface;
- running shoe: performance + worn → D2, diagonal sports editorial with one exact hero shoe unless a pair is visibly supplied;
- portable speaker: performance/identity + operated → D2, rugged outdoor audio or interior music design using wavefronts tied to grille/body geometry;
- cookware/tool: function + operated → D2, use-result triangle;
- furniture/home decor: comfort/status + installed → D1 or D2, environmental monument;
- pet product: convenience/comfort + used → D2, routine triangle with truthful animal interaction only when requested;
- toy/collectible: surprise/identity + displayed → D2 or D3, controlled reveal;
- medicine-like or health product: do not infer efficacy; use neutral packaging/lifestyle and supplied claims only;
- unknown industrial object: launch authority or functional proof, with conservative geometry and no invented specifications.


If the mechanism, density, and blueprint do not reinforce the same promise, reroute before generating.

Reject the record when `headline_axis=top-centered`, `product_axis=centered`, and `bottom_system=three-equal-cards`. That combination is the generic triptych, even when the background is attractive.
