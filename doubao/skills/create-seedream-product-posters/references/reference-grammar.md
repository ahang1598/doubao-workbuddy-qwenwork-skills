# Reference-Derived Poster Grammar

Use this reference to turn a casual product photo into a complete, high-impact Chinese or English commercial poster. The supplied examples are Chinese, but their hierarchy, six-band composition, density, rhythm, depth, and module grammar are language-independent. Copy the system, not the brand or literal copy.

## 1. What makes the references feel finished

The strongest references consistently combine five qualities:

1. **Immediate hook**: one headline or number dominates the first glance.
2. **Monumental product**: the sellable object is large, pristine, and surrounded by directed light or action.
3. **Commercial proof**: one dominant visible proof plus optional supporting benefits, a usage result, badge, price, or service strip explains why to believe.
4. **Designed depth**: foreground frame, hero, interaction effect, environment, and background cues create a real visual world.
5. **Deliberate closure**: a conversion CTA/offer, or a publicity slogan/brand strip, gives the eye an intentional ending.

A beautiful product image without product-specific proof and a deliberate close is an editorial still, not a complete commercial poster.

## 2. Six-band vertical anatomy

Map every 9:16 poster into six bands. Bands may overlap, but none should be accidental.

| Band | Typical height | Function | Common contents |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A Brand hook | 0–8% | ownership and campaign signal | logo, eyebrow, date, launch tag |
| B Hero promise | 8–27% | first attention anchor | 4–10 character headline, support line |
| C Proof rail | 25–38% | rational support | three benefits, icons, badge, mechanic |
| D Hero world | 34–78% | product desire and scene | product, usage result, character, interaction |
| E Action/close | 76–91% | action, offer, or publicity close | price, CTA, discount, availability, slogan |
| F Trust closure | 90–100% | reassurance and visual stop | service icons, slogan, legal note |

Reference-specific density:

- Premium hardware often expands Band D and keeps 7–9 modules.
- Beauty and oriental luxury use fewer containers but richer material and lighting.
- Food and beverage use a package/result triangle and 8–11 modules.
- Promotion/IP/social posters use 11–15 modules and stronger container contrast.

## 3. Attention-anchor rule

Define exactly three anchors:

1. **Hook anchor**: headline or campaign number.
2. **Desire anchor**: product, prepared result, person, or character.
3. **Action anchor**: CTA, price, or closing slogan.

The anchors should form a clear Z, vertical cascade, or triangle. Do not let badges, props, or benefit cards become a fourth equal anchor.

At mobile thumbnail size:

- Hook must remain readable or visibly dominant.
- Product silhouette must be recognizable.
- Action area must remain a distinct high-contrast shape.

## 4. Information-density budget

Count semantic modules, not every object.

### Premium density: 6–8 modules

Use for flagship tech, soft premium, and quiet luxury.

- brand/eyebrow;
- headline;
- support line;
- one benefit rail or three coordinated benefit cells counted as one system;
- hero product;
- one badge;
- CTA;
- optional trust/footer.

Richness comes from depth, material, and light—not more stickers.

### Commerce density: 8–11 modules

Use for beverage, food, home goods, and feature-led ecommerce.

- brand;
- headline;
- support line;
- benefit rail;
- product;
- usage result or interaction proof;
- badge;
- price/offer when verified;
- CTA;
- trust rail;
- optional seasonal or category cue.

### Campaign density: 11–15 modules

Use only for promotion, IP, and social storytelling.

- group every detail into three super-containers: hook, hero, conversion;
- use 3–6 annotations or price tags;
- repeat one visual motif to create order;
- preserve one calm contour around the hero.

More than 15 modules usually becomes noise. Fewer than 6 often feels like an unfinished mockup.

## 5. Visual cadence

The references alternate scale and quietness:

```text
small brand → huge headline → medium proof → huge product →
medium offer → compact trust closure
```

This `small–huge–medium–huge–medium–small` cadence creates drama without chaos.

Rules:

- use only two huge elements: headline and hero product;
- place a calm tonal field behind each text system;
- alternate filled and open zones; do not keep equal density from top to bottom;
- let one element cross a band boundary to integrate the composition;
- repeat alignment, border, icon, or color language at least three times;
- end with a full-width or centered visual stop so the poster feels complete.

## 6. Rich-scene construction

Use at least three, ideally four, of these five layers:

1. **Foreground**: blurred prop, cropped ingredient, glass edge, fabric, platform rim, desk object.
2. **Hero**: one dominant product or truthful product set.
3. **Interaction**: splash, airflow, orbit, hand action, pouring, motion path, usage result.
4. **Environment**: room, city, pavilion, arena, office, beach, restaurant, store world.
5. **Information**: headline, benefits, badge, conditional CTA or slogan, trust system.

Choose supporting elements by function:

- prove usage;
- echo product geometry;
- show scale;
- create depth;
- direct the eye;
- reinforce season or mood.

Reject props that serve none of these functions.

## 7. Container and motif systems

Choose one container family per poster:

- chamfered glass/metal panels for flagship tech;
- thin ornamental frames and seals for oriental luxury;
- rounded translucent chips for soft premium and refreshment;
- bold blocks, starbursts, and price cards for promotions;
- torn paper, tape, speech bubbles, and faux notes for social/IP;
- angled black bars and technical tags for sport.

Do not mix glass panels, antique seals, comic starbursts, and torn paper in one poster unless the concept is deliberately maximalist.

Repeat one motif three to five times:

- circle/lens/orbit;
- arch/moon gate;
- diagonal slash;
- rounded pill;
- paper tear;
- rectangular price tag;
- thin ornamental border.

## 8. Category-to-scene transformation

For an arbitrary snapshot, derive the world from visible product cues:

| Visible cue | Useful world |
|---|---|
| dark reflective electronics | night architecture, optical portal, precision stage |
| white/light portable device | high-key pavilion, airflow fabric, glass, summer daylight |
| green circular-camera phone | jade optics, museum moon gate, glass mountains |
| orange wearable | track, city editorial, speed grid, energy pulse |
| stainless vacuum bottle | thermal diptych, brushed-steel light arc, condensation boundary |
| floral dress | asymmetric fashion set, textile macro, hem-shadow movement |
| running shoe | outsole-derived lanes, impact shadow, environmental speed |
| portable speaker | grille-derived wavefronts, vibration light, responsive campsite surfaces |
| pink/clear beverage | fruit splash, ice, bubbles, high-key seasonal world |
| warm food/coffee package | home ritual, breakfast table, hand interaction |
| ornate cosmetic package | silk, flowers, carved frame, refined portrait |
| multiple discounted SKUs | tiered sale arena, price tags, full-width mechanics |

Use the product’s shape as a visual metaphor whenever possible: circular camera → moon gate; round earbuds case → acoustic orbit; fan → airflow ribbon; bottle → splash tower.

## 9. Anti-template rule

The references often contain brand, headline, proof, hero and closure, but their completeness must not be reduced to one reusable stack. Reject the combination `top-centered headline + centered isolated product + three equal bottom cards`. It remains generic even when the background, palette, or category changes.

For each concept, keep the six-band logic while changing the composition axis, overlap, proof mechanism, module shapes, scene interaction, and bottom close. Require two devices that could not be pasted unchanged onto an unrelated product.

## 10. Originality rule

When a supplied reference is used:

- keep its macro grammar, density, and visual cadence;
- change brand identifiers, exact copy, product, scene details, and decorative drawing;
- retain at most one signature layout device;
- introduce two product-specific devices derived from shape, material, color, or use.

The result should feel equally complete and commercially forceful, not like a traced campaign.
