## 1. Tech flagship

**Best for:** phones, cars, computers, appliances, premium hardware, B2B technology.

**Promise:** performance, precision, authority, the future.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: black, midnight navy, graphite, silver; one cyan, electric blue, or champagne-gold accent.
- Background: dark studio, future city, light tunnel, monumental architecture, glossy stage.
- Light: sharp rim light, controlled highlights, luminous horizontal horizon, reflective floor.
- Product: large, isolated, three-quarter angle, lower-middle of the frame; keep at least one clean contour.
- Type: oversized condensed sans or metal-gradient display title; tracking for technical labels.
- Motifs: laser lines, glass panels, hexagonal icons, data strips, subtle glow.

**Layout**

- Brand/eyebrow at top 3–8%.
- Hero title at 10–25%.
- Three benefits at 26–34%.
- Product at 38–76%.
- Performance strip, conversion CTA, or publicity slogan at 78–91%.
- Service items at 92–98%.

**Avoid:** rainbow neon, excessive HUD noise, tiny car/product, random circuit patterns, fake specifications.

### Flagship launch: rich mode

Use this enhanced mode when the poster must feel like a major launch event rather than a minimal catalog image.

- Build five visible depth layers: metallic headline flare, disciplined information cards, hero-product halo, receding architectural portal, and foreground launch stage.
- Use 7–10 information modules total. Count the headline block, feature row, hero stage, badge, CTA-or-slogan close, and footer as modules; do not fill every gap.
- Make the hero product occupy 55–70% of canvas width and overlap the portal so it feels monumental.
- Use one dominant proof module plus one or two subordinate callouts. Equal cards are allowed only when they are not paired with a top-centered headline, centered product, and bottom triptych.
- Add one sharp horizontal energy line beneath the headline and one waveform, orbit, or light-track system behind the product.
- Use product color as the only strong accent: cyan for dark navy, aqua for white, emerald for green, amber-orange for orange. Keep titanium, graphite, and cool white as the shared family palette.
- Create richness through foreground/midground/background separation, bevels, reflections, controlled particles, and volumetric light. Never use random glyphs or a wall of HUD details.
- Keep one area calm behind every text block and one clean contour around the product.

## 2. Soft premium

**Best for:** cosmetics, beauty devices, fragrance, gifts, women-oriented appliances.

**Promise:** refinement, comfort, gentle efficacy, gifting value.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: warm ivory, blush, champagne, rose gold, cocoa.
- Background: soft-focus vanity, silk, flowers, curved mirror, bright interior.
- Light: large softbox, diffuse glow, pearl-like highlights, low contrast.
- Product: pristine, tactile, close enough to show finish; use rounded shadows.
- Type: elegant high-contrast serif for the headline plus light sans for facts.
- Motifs: fine-line icons, seals, floral outlines, translucent cards.

**Layout**

- Use generous breathing room.
- Keep the headline to one or two lines.
- Use three small benefit icons in a single row.
- For conversion, place a price or CTA in a soft rounded card, never a harsh starburst. For publicity, use an unboxed slogan or thin editorial rule.

**Avoid:** heavy black outlines, saturated red, crowded badges, strong sci-fi glow.

### Portrait-product dual proof

Use for foundation, powder, lipstick, fragrance, and beauty devices when a person or visible use result is commercially relevant and authorized.

- Let the portrait occupy one side or the upper background; product close-up occupies the opposite lower half.
- Connect face and product through gaze, hand, mirror, silk fold, floral branch, or shared highlight direction.
- Use 6–9 modules. The person is result proof, not a separate campaign story.
- Keep the product at least 35% of canvas height and its packaging fully legible.
- Use one refined badge and either one conversion CTA or one publicity slogan; do not surround the face with multiple promotional stickers.
- For ornate Chinese beauty, use ivory, pale peach, antique gold, fine floral engraving, Song-style headline, and thin framed icons.

Avoid unauthorized faces, excessive skin retouching, medical efficacy claims, and a portrait so dominant that the product becomes a prop.

## 3. Oriental quiet luxury

**Best for:** tea, Chinese cosmetics, cultural gifts, incense, premium food, heritage brands.

**Promise:** cultural depth, craftsmanship, calm quality.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: parchment, tea brown, ink black, celadon, dark green, muted red, antique gold.
- Background: moon gate, wood table, lattice window, bamboo shadow, misty mountain, silk or handmade paper.
- Light: warm side light, late-afternoon sun, lantern glow, natural falloff.
- Product: centered like an exhibit, grounded on wood or stone.
- Type: Song-style serif or restrained calligraphic headline; modern sans only for supporting facts.
- Motifs: thin borders, seals, vertical inscriptions, cloud/flower geometry, landscape linework.

**Layout**

- Maintain symmetry or a calm left-right balance.
- Use one large cultural headline, one refined subline, and three framed benefits.
- Keep ornaments thin and secondary.

**Avoid:** costume-drama clutter unless the objective is social storytelling, random ancient characters, excessive gold bevels, fake historical claims.

## 4. Refreshing beverage

**Best for:** coffee, soda, water, juice, dairy, seasonal drinks.

**Promise:** thirst relief, flavor, cold freshness, seasonal delight.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: product-color-led plus white; sky blue/cobalt for tropical freshness, pink for peach/floral, yellow for citrus.
- Background: bright poolside, beach, condensation studio, fruit garden, ice world.
- Light: high-key sunlight, transparent refraction, crisp specular highlights.
- Product: 45–60% of height, centered or slightly right, with visible droplets and ice.
- Type: bold energetic sans, occasional brush or handwritten accent.
- Motifs: splash arcs, ice cubes, sliced fruit, bubbles, coconut/leaf cues, circular seasonal badge.

**Layout**

- H1 occupies 10–24%.
- Three flavor/benefit chips stack left or form one row.
- Price sits in a solid contrast tile.
- A conversion CTA uses a large pill or rounded rectangle near 82–90%; a publicity poster uses no button and closes with a slogan.

**Avoid:** covering the label with splashes, impossible liquid physics, too many fruits, low-contrast white copy over foam.

### Transparent-drink truth lock

Record cup outline, lid/rim, fill level, liquid gradient, label position, number and placement of visible fruit slices, ice scale, condensation, and transparent gaps. Keep splashes behind or around the cup; never let them erase the label or make the liquid cross the cup wall.

## 5. Daily lifestyle

**Best for:** coffee concentrate, snacks, breakfast products, home goods, kitchen products, restaurants.

**Promise:** easy everyday use, appetite, familiarity, a better routine.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: warm cream, food browns, brand red, natural wood, soft sunlight.
- Background: breakfast table, home kitchen, office desk, restaurant gathering.
- Light: window light or warm practical lighting; appetite-enhancing highlights.
- Product: show both pack and usage result when truthful: box + prepared drink, package + bowl, ingredient + serving.
- Type: clear bold headline with friendly sans support.
- Motifs: hand interaction, checklist icons, calendar or routine cues.

**Layout**

- Put headline and three benefits above the hero scene.
- Use foreground food or props to frame, not block, the pack.
- CTA and trust bar for conversion, or slogan and brand close for publicity, occupy the bottom 12–18%.

**Avoid:** sterile catalog feeling, too much empty luxury space, food that misrepresents the contents.

### Pack-result-routine triangle

Use this conversion-focused subtype for snacks, breakfast products, coffee concentrate, and portable food packs.

- Anchor 1: oversized routine promise, such as daily, breakfast, or ready-in-seconds.
- Anchor 2: truthful pack system—outer box, open pack, or one hero sachet.
- Anchor 3: prepared or serving result, such as bowl, milk, coffee, or cup.
- Add one human action only when it proves convenience: taking a sachet from a bag, pouring, opening, or serving.
- Build a triangle among hero pack, usage result, and CTA. The pack remains larger or more central than loose ingredients.
- Use 9–11 modules: brand, headline, support banner, benefit rail, pack, result, small badge, CTA, optional verified quantity, trust closure.
- Repeat brand color across headline, package, CTA, and one badge. Keep the room warm and secondary.

Avoid inventing ingredients, showing more contents than the pack supports, or turning a single-pack product into a gift set.

## 6. Fashion editorial

**Best for:** dresses, apparel, bags, jewelry, footwear launches where silhouette, fabric, and identity matter more than specifications.

**Promise:** a distinctive look, material desire, and a believable wearing mood.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: garment-led neutrals plus one seasonal accent; preserve print colors exactly.
- Background: studio cyclorama, sunlit architecture, fashion-set paper, botanical shadow, hotel corridor, or a tightly art-directed location.
- Light: directional editorial light that reveals drape, weave, translucency, seams, and edge movement.
- Product: use one asymmetric hero silhouette. A flat garment needs a visible hanger/mannequin or a clearly intentional invisible-form studio construction.
- Type: high-contrast editorial serif plus restrained grotesk; captions can run vertically or along a margin.
- Motifs: fabric ribbon, cropped material macro, seam callout, contact sheet, measuring rule, shadow frame.

**Layout**

- Avoid a centered catalog cutout beneath a centered headline.
- Use a 55/45 or 65/35 asymmetric split, edge crop, diagonal hem movement, or editorial contact-sheet composition.
- Add one magnified fabric/print detail and one use-context cue; these replace generic benefit cards.
- Close publicity posters with a season line, collection name, or quiet editorial caption—not a button.

**Avoid:** unsupported body-shaping claims, fabricated fabric names, invented colorways, extra garments, random flowers that hide the print, implausible floating straps, and three equal icon pills.

## 7. Sport energy

**Best for:** shoes, sportswear, outdoor gear, fitness products.

**Promise:** movement, responsiveness, confidence.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: black/white plus one electric accent such as volt yellow, orange, or cyan.
- Background: wet city street, track, concrete architecture, speed tunnel.
- Light: hard sun or high-contrast edge light; motion streaks.
- Product: exaggerated diagonal angle, 45–65% of frame, sharp while the environment moves.
- Type: condensed italic or block display, slashed bars, angled copy cards.
- Motifs: paint streaks, speed lines, technical mesh callouts.

**Layout**

- Use a 6–12° diagonal energy axis.
- Keep benefits in a dark horizontal technical strip.
- Balance one oversized product with one smaller action scene.

**Avoid:** distorting sole geometry, fake technology names, placing the product at an impossible perspective.

## 8. Playful IP

**Best for:** blind boxes, mascots, youth food and drinks, campus campaigns, social merchandise.

**Promise:** surprise, belonging, humor, collectability.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: saturated brand color, black/white contrast, one complementary accent.
- Background: stage, desk collage, store world, sticker wall.
- Light: glossy toy photography or bright editorial collage.
- Product: central reveal, open-box burst, mascot interaction, visible hero pack.
- Type: chunky outlined display lettering, torn paper, stickers, hand-drawn marks.
- Motifs: starbursts, crowns, emoji-like faces, tape, confetti, speech bubbles.

**Layout**

- Allow controlled density.
- Keep one unmistakable headline zone and one central hero silhouette.
- Use 3–5 stickers, not a sticker on every gap.

**Avoid:** making the mascot more important than the sellable product, inconsistent character design, illegible overlapping labels.

### IP role hierarchy

Assign every character depiction one role:

1. one main 3D mascot that points toward, holds, reveals, or celebrates the product;
2. package-printed mascot/artwork that remains part of product truth;
3. optional one flat sticker or doodle near the headline.

Do not count package printing as a duplicate character, but do forbid a second 3D mascot unless a group campaign explicitly requires it. Keep character gesture or gaze directed toward the product or CTA.

## 9. Promotion arena

**Best for:** shopping festivals, multi-SKU sales, home appliances, price-driven retail.

**Promise:** urgency, deal clarity, assortment, platform trust.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: campaign red, white, black, metallic gold; a small amount of dark stage depth.
- Background: illuminated retail arena, concentric platform, sale hall, high-energy rays.
- Light: glossy highlights and red rim lights.
- Product: arrange 3–7 SKUs on tiered platforms, with larger anchors behind and small items in front.
- Type: huge 3D sale headline, white/red price cards, bold numerals.
- Motifs: banners, price tags, discount bars, delivery and guarantee icons.

**Layout**

- Campaign mark and date at 0–8%.
- Sale headline at 8–30%.
- Product grid at 32–70%.
- Discount mechanic at 72–82%.
- CTA/delivery at 83–91%.
- Trust strip at 92–99%.

**Avoid:** invented prices, conflicting discount mechanics, more than seven hero SKUs, tiny unreadable product labels.

### SKU hierarchy pyramid

Use a three-tier product pyramid instead of distributing every item evenly:

1. **Anchor tier:** one or two large appliances/products, 45–60% of hero-zone height.
2. **Support tier:** two or three medium products, 25–40%.
3. **Accessory tier:** one or two small products, 15–25%.

Give each SKU one adjacent exact-copy price card. Never let price cards cross products or float ambiguously. Group all deal mechanics into one full-width bar below the products, then one logistics/CTA bar, then one trust rail. This produces `campaign headline → product pyramid → deal mechanic → delivery CTA → trust` without confusion.

Multi-SKU posters require two-pass exact-copy mode whenever prices or discount arithmetic appear.

## 10. Social story collage

**Best for:** office memes, personality-led brands, cultural skits, community campaigns, restaurant emotions.

**Promise:** recognition, humor, emotion, shareability.

**Visual grammar**

- Palette: theme-led; office blue/white/yellow, Chinese ink/red, nightlife black/gold.
- Background: a complete narrative scene, not a neutral product stage.
- Character: central human expression or group moment; product remains a story prop with clear brand visibility.
- Type: editorial headline plus handwritten, taped, or notification-style microcopy.
- Motifs: notes, chat cards, task lists, arrows, faux UI, torn paper, brush labels.

**Layout**

- Reserve 25–35% for the headline and hook.
- Put the character/product story in the center 45–55%.
- Use 3–6 annotation cards around the subject.
- Close with one slogan or CTA.

**Avoid:** fake app notifications that imply real people, overcrowded text, using a face without authorization, losing the product.

## 11. Selection matrix

| Objective | Primary family | Optional secondary | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| New premium hardware | Tech flagship | Promotion arena 10–20% | Medium |
| Beauty launch | Soft premium | Oriental quiet luxury | Low |
| Heritage tea/cosmetics | Oriental quiet luxury | Social story collage | Low–medium |
| Summer beverage | Refreshing beverage | Playful IP | Medium |
| Breakfast/daily use | Daily lifestyle | Promotion arena | Medium |
| Apparel/fashion launch | Fashion editorial | Soft premium or Social story collage | Low–medium |
| Athletic performance | Sport energy | Tech flagship | Medium |
| Blind box reveal | Playful IP | Promotion arena | High |
| Shopping festival | Promotion arena | Tech flagship | High |
| Meme/social campaign | Social story collage | Playful IP | High but organized |

Choose the family by the buying reason, not by the product category alone.

## 12. Batch diversity router

When generating posters for multiple unrelated products, do not default every item to the tech-flagship system. Assign contrasting art directions before writing prompts.

Use these field-tested directions:

### High-key crystal lifestyle

**Best for:** white portable appliances, summer devices, clean beauty hardware.

- Palette: pearl white, ice blue, aqua, transparent glass, a tiny warm-sun accent.
- Scene: sunlit pavilion, acrylic waves, fabric in airflow, droplets, refraction, leaf shadow.
- Type: elegant modern serif or light geometric sans.
- Layout: asymmetric editorial split; stacked translucent labels; no dark portal or launch stage.
- Richness: glass foreground, flowing fabric midground, bright architectural arches in depth.

### Contemporary Chinese jade optical

**Best for:** green electronics, imaging products, premium cultural-tech concepts.

- Palette: ink black, jade green, celadon, ivory, brushed bronze, one cinnabar seal.
- Scene: moon gate that echoes a lens, translucent mountain glass, lacquer stone, museum spotlight.
- Type: high-contrast Chinese serif with restrained vertical copy.
- Layout: quiet asymmetric exhibition composition; narrow caption labels; slim CTA only for conversion, otherwise an unboxed slogan close.
- Richness: optical rings, jade slabs, mist layers, cultural material contrast; no generic cyber tunnel.

### Urban sports editorial

**Best for:** smart bands, headphones, shoes, portable youth electronics.

- Palette: black, warm off-white, fluorescent orange or volt yellow, small metallic accent.
- Scene: track grid, asphalt, city strip, tape, halftone, speed marks.
- Type: oversized condensed display, diagonal slashes, magazine tags.
- Layout: 6–14° energy axis; product breaks frames; tags replace glass cards.
- Richness: paper grain, cropped arrows, motion echoes, lane markings; no circular launch platform.

### Distance test

For every pair in a batch, change at least four dimensions: brightness, palette, layout axis, type personality, material/scene, and density/motifs. A palette swap alone fails.
