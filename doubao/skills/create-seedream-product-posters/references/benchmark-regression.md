# Poster Skill Regression Suite

Use these sixteen cases after meaningful changes to the workflow. They test arbitrary snapshots, product fidelity, text discipline, layout richness, category routing, batch diversity, anti-template behavior, claim provenance, and two-pass exact copy.

## Case A: dark spherical open-fit earbuds

**Input risk:** tiny dark product, unusual spherical case, two asymmetric earbuds, shallow lid recess that can become a false object.

**Expected route:** low-key flagship audio launch; centered monument; metallic headline; acoustic portal; 7–9 modules.

**Required locks:**

- one spherical case only;
- glossy upper lid and matte lower half;
- shallow lid recess remains empty;
- one centered indicator and one visible charging port;
- exactly two separate open-fit earbuds;
- no third earbud or bump on the case.

**Known failure:** the first pass turned the recess into an earbud-shaped blob. Negative-geometry revision was required.

## Case B: white handheld fan

**Input risk:** simple white object can disappear into a high-key scene or be misread as a hair dryer.

**Expected route:** high-key crystal summer lifestyle; asymmetric layout; airflow fabric, acrylic waves, sea/pavilion depth; rounded chips; 8–10 modules.

**Required locks:**

- one cylindrical handle;
- one angled cylindrical fan head;
- circular grille, centered hub, visible radial blades;
- exactly two visible controls;
- no nozzle, cord, display, or duplicate device.

**Pass signal:** product remains crisp while the rich scene uses airflow, transparent material, daylight, and foreground depth instead of dark technology tropes.

## Case C: orange woven smart band

**Input risk:** narrow product can become too small; strap texture and pill body are easy to change; hand from source must not be copied into a poster unless desired.

**Expected route:** orange-black urban sports editorial; diagonal axis; torn-paper frame; city-running strip; pulse line; 8–10 modules.

**Required locks:**

- exactly one pill-shaped black display;
- narrow silver frame;
- orange woven strap of consistent width and texture;
- no extra buttons, sensors, duplicate product, or hand.

**Pass signal:** huge readable product, organized high density, screen/strap/pulse/CTA share one orange rhythm, and no flagship glass-card stack is reused.

## Case D: packaged daily nuts

**Input risk:** box, sachets, ingredients, serving result, and hand action can compete; quantity and ingredients can be hallucinated.

**Expected route:** high-key pack-result-routine triangle; warm kitchen; friendly bold headline; rounded benefit rail; red conversion dock; 9–11 modules.

**Required locks:**

- exactly one open box and one large hero sachet;
- visible package colors and product name remain unchanged;
- serving result uses only visibly supported ingredients;
- one convenience action at most;
- source package text stays on the package and does not become background copy.

**Pass signal:** product pack is the largest commercial proof, the serving result increases appetite, and the CTA closes the triangle without turning ingredients into equal heroes.

## Case E: ornate square pressed powder

**Input risk:** mirror, hinge, powder pan, embossed relief, clasp, and empty transparent gaps can merge or become extra objects.

**Expected route:** soft premium plus oriental quiet luxury; high-key silk pavilion; asymmetric exhibit; 6–9 modules.

**Required locks:**

- exactly one open rounded-square compact;
- square mirror lid, hinge, front clasp, powder pan, and floral relief stay distinct;
- mirror area and gaps remain empty/reflective;
- no round-case substitution, extra cosmetics, or unauthorized person.

**Pass signal:** material, embossing, silk, flower, and warm light provide richness while the copy system stays quiet and refined.

## Case F: six-SKU appliance group

**Input risk:** category substitution, duplicate appliances, equal visual weight, ambiguous price cards, and invented discounts.

**Expected route:** red promotion arena; anchor/support/accessory product pyramid; 13–15 modules grouped into three super-containers.

**Required locks:**

- exactly one refrigerator, washer, air conditioner, television, robot vacuum, and air fryer;
- no product is hidden or replaced;
- one blank price-tag shape per SKU for later exact typesetting;
- no prices, discount arithmetic, platform logo, or unverified brand claim in the generated key visual.

**Pass signal:** all six categories remain identifiable at thumbnail size, product tiers are obvious, and the deal bar, CTA, and trust rail form three distinct lower bands.

## Case G: branded iced lemon tea with mascot

**Input risk:** transparent cup physics, label occlusion, duplicated character, fruit/ice exaggeration, invented price or volume, and uncontrolled comic density.

**Expected route:** playful IP plus refreshing beverage; diagonal comic burst; red-white-yellow palette; 11–13 modules grouped into three super-containers.

**Required locks:**

- exactly one transparent hero cup with plausible wall, rim, golden liquid, fill line, ice and lemon slices;
- visible cup wordmark remains on the product;
- splashes and ice frame the cup without crossing the wall or label;
- exactly one main 3D mascot;
- package-printed mascot remains part of product truth;
- at most one additional flat mascot sticker/doodle;
- no price, discount, volume, certification, or store claim unless supplied.

**Pass signal:** headline, drink, and CTA remain obvious at thumbnail size; the mascot directs attention toward the drink; all annotations belong to headline, hero world, or conversion dock.

## Case H: weak-light transparent serum bottle

**Input risk:** crushed shadows, bathroom clutter, glare, tilted perspective, transparent glass and liquid boundary collapse.

**Expected route:** amber quiet-luxury laboratory; centered exhibit; low-density gold information system; 7–9 modules.

**Required locks:** exactly one rectangular clear bottle; outer wall, inner amber liquid boundary, fill level and thick glass base stay distinct; one black dropper; one centered label; no opaque substitution, extra applicator, invented ingredient or efficacy.

**Pass signal:** the bottle stays transparent and structurally plausible while edge light, backlit glass, botanical shadow and foreground refraction provide depth.

## Case I: single running shoe in a cluttered entryway

**Input risk:** clutter, ambiguous footwear in the distance, altered sole geometry, duplicate shoe, fake branding and unsupported technology.

**Expected route:** black-white-volt urban sports editorial; diagonal axis; torn paper and wet-city motion; 8–10 modules.

**Required locks:** exactly one hero shoe; black mesh upper, lime lining/laces, white sculpted midsole and black outsole; coherent lace path, opening, toe and heel; no logo, second shoe, foot, air unit, extra strap or fake technology.

**Pass signal:** the product is sharp and monumental while all motion stays in the environment, runner silhouette and graphics.

## Case J: glare-heavy flexible cosmetic tube

**Input risk:** soft packaging becomes a rigid bottle, top crimp disappears, cap changes, glare hides the label, or extra packaging appears.

**Expected route:** high-key soft-premium floral editorial; asymmetric silk landscape; 6–9 modules.

**Required locks:** exactly one pearl-pink flexible tube; sealed ribbed crimp, soft shoulders and plausible wrinkles; brushed gold cylindrical screw cap; centered minimal label; no jar, pump, box, person, second tube or invented ingredient.

**Pass signal:** the tube remains visibly flexible while silk, magnolia, curved mirror and shallow depth create richness.

## Case K: real handheld fan with two-pass exact copy

**Input risk:** small low-resolution product, white-on-bright separation, hair-dryer substitution, extra control, generated pseudo-text, and overlay coordinates drifting from planned blank cards.

**Expected route:** high-key crystal lifestyle; asymmetric editorial split; text-free key visual followed by deterministic Chinese overlay; 9 modules.

**Required locks:** one white cylindrical handle; one angled circular fan head; recessed grille, centered hub and radial blades; exactly two visible controls; no nozzle, cord, display, duplicate, pseudo-text or placeholder glyph.

**Pass signal:** preflight passes; key visual contains no poster text; overlay strings match the manifest; actual blank-container positions are measured after generation; CTA uses font-supported characters; QA gate reaches at least 90 with no hard rejection.

**Recorded result:** 96/100 — product 28, message 24, layout 19, art direction 15, trust/finish 10.

## Case L: English publicity poster without CTA

**Input risk:** literal translation, accidental Chinese overlay copy, broken English words, inconsistent capitalization, generic `Explore Now` button, and a bottom area that feels unfinished after CTA removal.

**Expected route:** one style family appropriate to the product; `output_language=en`; `requires_cta=false`; exact English manifest; non-interactive slogan close.

**Required locks:** preserve all physical product labels in their source language; English headline uses no more than eight words; benefits use no more than six words each; every English line breaks at word boundaries; no button, arrow, search bar, purchase instruction, `Explore Now`, invented offer, or mixed-language overlay copy.

**Pass signal:** headline and product are the first two anchors; the final anchor is a clear English brand/theme slogan integrated with the scene or stage; typesetting uses a verified Latin font and natural word-boundary wrapping; the bottom remains intentional without appearing clickable.

## Case M: stainless vacuum bottle with unsupported category claims

**Input risk:** generic office/mountain lifestyle, steam and ice on the same undivided bottle, hidden cap/body redesign, and invented steel grade, thermal hours, capacity, leakproofing or anti-scald claims.

**Expected route:** D1 or D2 material-proof still life or a deliberate hot/cold diptych; asymmetric axis; brushed-steel light sculpture; no bottom triptych.

**Required locks:** one exact bottle; cap height, shoulder, taper, metal grain, seam and base preserved; one dominant thermal metaphor or a visibly divided two-zone story; every factual line marked visible or user-supplied.

**Pass signal:** two product-specific devices are visible, temperature effects are physically coherent, the product remains the first or second anchor, and no unsupported specification appears.

## Case N: floral dress without model

**Input risk:** floating catalog cutout, changed strap/neckline/print, invented fabric and body-shaping claims, duplicated colorway, and generic three-pill footer.

**Expected route:** fashion editorial; asymmetric 55/45 or 65/35 split; plausible hanger/mannequin/invisible-form support; textile macro and hem movement; D1 or D2.

**Required locks:** exact garment count, strap path, neckline, waist, seams, print repeat, translucency and hem; no model or extra garment unless supplied.

**Pass signal:** support is believable, one material macro proves textile character, the bottom closes as a season/collection caption, and no “slimming,” “breathable,” or “multiway” claim appears without provenance.

## Case O: single running shoe with generic performance copy

**Input risk:** an extra second shoe, redrawn brand/logo, invented weight or cushioning technology, static product with disconnected speed lines, and three equal feature cards.

**Expected route:** diagonal sports editorial; one exact hero shoe; outsole-derived lane motif, impact shadow, surface interaction, or environmental motion; D2.

**Required locks:** exact count, upper, lace path, tongue/opening, midsole, outsole, toe and heel; no numeric claim or named technology unless user-supplied.

**Pass signal:** movement originates in the scene and product interaction, the shoe stays structurally sharp, and the layout signature is not top-centered/centered/three-equal-cards.

## Case P: portable speaker at a campsite

**Input risk:** campfire/tent overtake the product, floating music-note clip art, altered grille/buttons/strap, and invented IP rating, play time, TWS, surround, wattage or driver claims.

**Expected route:** rugged outdoor audio; asymmetric product monument; grille-derived acoustic wavefronts and environmental light response; D2.

**Required locks:** exact grille weave, proportions, end caps, buttons, ports, strap hardware, feet and logo; all factual benefits have provenance.

**Pass signal:** speaker is the anchor, fire/tent remain context, acoustic effects emerge from product geometry, and no unsupported specification appears.

## Batch gate

The suite passes only when:

- every tested image scores at least 90/100 with no hard rejection;
- each pair differs in at least four of six style-distance dimensions;
- compared batch outputs use different container families;
- all visible copy belongs to the prompt’s exact-copy manifest;
- the product remains the first or second attention anchor;
- each poster has an intentional bottom closure.

If one output fails, revise the failed dimension and record the failure in [execution-patterns.md](execution-patterns.md) before repackaging the skill.
