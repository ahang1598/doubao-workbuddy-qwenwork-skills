# Vertical Product Poster Layout System

Use normalized percentages so the design scales across output sizes.

## 1. Canvas and safe zones

Default canvas: mobile vertical `9:16`, Seedream 5.0 Pro `2K` (commonly `1584x2816` when mapped by the model).

- Outer safe margin: 5–6% of width.
- Top platform-safe zone: 3% of height.
- Bottom platform-safe zone: 4% of height.
- When a conversion CTA is used, keep it at least 4% above the bottom edge. Publicity posters use a non-interactive slogan or brand close instead.
- Keep critical text on calm, high-contrast surfaces.
- Use one dominant vertical axis. Allow diagonal axes only for sport, IP, or promotion styles.

## 2. Core hierarchy

The poster must be readable in this order within two seconds:

1. What is the promise?
2. What is the product?
3. Why should I believe it?
4. What should I remember, or what should I do when conversion is intended?

Default scale relationships:

- Hero headline: 100% reference size.
- Supporting line: 30–42%.
- Benefit title: 20–28%.
- Benefit explanation: 13–18%.
- Price numeral: 70–110% when price is the objective.
- CTA for conversion or slogan close for publicity: 28–40%.
- Trust/legal line: 10–14%.

Use one hero headline. Avoid two elements competing at the same scale.

## 3. Six layout formulas

### Formula A: Flagship monument

Use for technology, cars, premium appliances, and hero launches.

| Zone | Height | Content |
|---|---:|---|
| Brand/eyebrow | 3–8% | Logo or short launch label |
| Hero title | 9–25% | Large headline and support line |
| Proof zone | 26–34% | One dominant proof plus up to two subordinate callouts |
| Product stage | 36–76% | One large product, reflections/background |
| Action/close | 78–91% | Conversion CTA, performance strip, or publicity slogan |
| Trust bar | 92–98% | Up to three service items |

Product width: 58–80% of canvas. Preserve negative space around the outer contour.

For **flagship launch: rich mode**, use this normalized stack:

- 3–7%: brand eyebrow;
- 8–22%: oversized metallic headline and one horizontal flare;
- 23–28%: concise support line;
- 29–37%: one dominant glass-metal proof module plus one or two subordinate callouts;
- 38–77%: hero product, halo, waveform/orbit, and architectural portal;
- 72–80%: one small badge attached to the hero zone;
- 81–94%: beveled CTA for conversion, or slogan plus extended foreground stage for publicity;
- 95–98%: optional single quiet footer line only.

Do not combine a top-centered headline, centered product, and three equal cards. Shift at least one major axis or proof mechanism so the layout is product-specific. Let diagonal energy appear only in light tracks and reflections when it supports the object.

### Formula B: Editorial split

Use for beauty, lifestyle, or a scene with a person.

- Place copy in the upper-left or left 42–52% of the canvas.
- Place the person or product on the right and lower center.
- Use a curved mirror, arch, moon gate, or window as a spatial separator.
- Keep the headline between 10–28%.
- Put the product close-up between 40–78%.
- Use one price/CTA card near 80–92% for conversion, or one quiet slogan/brand line for publicity.

Ensure the gaze, hand, or scene line points toward the product and, when used, the CTA.

### Formula C: Product plus usage result

Use for coffee, snacks, food, or home routines.

- Headline and benefits: 5–32%.
- Package/hero product: 38–72%.
- Prepared result or in-use proof: 50–78%, secondary to the pack.
- Conversion CTA and trust strip, or publicity slogan and brand strip: 82–98%.

For conversion, create a triangle between package, usage result, and CTA. For publicity, let package and result lead to a slogan close without drawing a button.

For packaged food, divide the hero world into three unequal masses:

- 45–55% visual weight: hero box/bag/bottle;
- 20–30%: one serving result;
- 10–20%: one action or ingredient frame;
- remaining weight: calm tabletop and depth.

The package label must be the sharpest small-detail region.

### Formula D: Refreshment tower

Use for beverages.

- Headline: 7–24%.
- Benefit chips: left stack at 30–52% or row at 26–34%.
- Hero drink: 36–78%, 45–60% of total poster height.
- Ingredient proof: around the base, no more than three ingredient types.
- Price: contrast tile at 68–80%.
- Conversion CTA, or publicity slogan without a button: centered at 82–91%.
- Trust strip: 92–98%.

Keep the product label unobstructed and the liquid level physically plausible.

### Formula E: Dynamic diagonal

Use for sport and youth products.

- Tilt the visual axis 6–12°.
- Put the headline in the upper 25–32% with angled underline or brush bar.
- Float the product across the middle 38–72% at a strong three-quarter angle.
- Put a smaller action scene in the lower background.
- Use one technical benefit strip at 28–38%.
- Put conversion CTA/trust or publicity slogan/trust at 86–97%.

Keep the product itself sharp. Apply motion only to background or secondary action.

### Formula F: Dense campaign stack

Use only for promotion arena, playful IP, or social story collage.

- Hook/brand: 0–8%.
- Oversized campaign headline: 8–28%.
- Offer mechanics or stickers: 26–38%.
- Hero products/character: 35–73%.
- Price or conversion mechanic: 72–84%.
- CTA/delivery for conversion, or campaign slogan for publicity: 84–92%.
- Trust/legal: 92–99%.

Create three containers—headline, hero, conversion—before adding any decorative stickers. Every annotation must attach visually to one container.

For multi-SKU, subdivide the hero container into an anchor/support/accessory pyramid. Place each price card within one product-width of its SKU and connect by proximity, not decorative leader lines.

### Formula G: Portrait-product dual proof

Use for beauty, personal care, and authorized model-led launches.

- 0–8%: brand or ornamental eyebrow;
- 8–27%: elegant headline and support;
- 25–36%: three restrained proof icons;
- 30–62%: portrait/result proof on one side;
- 48–80%: product close-up on the opposite lower side, crossing the portrait boundary;
- 76–91%: one badge plus conversion CTA, or one publicity slogan;
- 92–98%: quiet descriptor or trust closure.

Keep one diagonal relationship among face, hand/gaze, and product. Do not center both face and product on the same axis.

## 4. Typography

Use at most two type personalities:

- premium/tech: condensed sans or geometric sans + neutral sans;
- soft premium: high-contrast serif + light sans;
- oriental: Song-style serif or restrained calligraphy + modern sans;
- sport: condensed italic/block display + sans;
- IP/promotion: chunky display + bold sans;
- social collage: editorial display + handwritten accent.

Rules:

- Use display type only for the hero line.
- Keep a consistent numerical style.
- Use outlines/shadows only when the background requires separation.
- Use metallic gradients only for premium technology or luxury accents.
- Do not make body copy 3D.
- Use Chinese punctuation sparingly in short Chinese headline copy.
- For English display copy, use 1–8 headline words and control case explicitly. Avoid a full-width block of all caps when the headline exceeds four words.
- Break English at word boundaries. Keep articles, prepositions, phrasal verbs, numbers with units, and brand/model names together when possible.
- Give English text wider boxes than Chinese text: start with 8–12% more horizontal room or reduce the type size before forcing an awkward extra line.

## 5. Density and spacing

Define density by information modules, not object count:

- Low density: 4–6 modules. Premium and quiet luxury.
- Medium density: 7–10 modules. Most ecommerce posters.
- High density: 11–15 modules. Promotion, IP, social collage only.

One module may be a headline block, benefit row, product stage, price card, conditional CTA, slogan, or trust bar.

Use the six-band and visual-cadence method in [reference-grammar.md](reference-grammar.md). A normal commercial poster should have visible changes in scale and density from band to band; equal-sized modules stacked uniformly look like a template.

Spacing:

- Gap between major modules: 2.5–5% of canvas height.
- Gap within a copy block: 0.6–1.5%.
- Corner radius system: choose one family—sharp/chamfered, soft rounded, or ornamental. Do not mix all three.
- Icon stroke and border weight must match.

## 6. Copy-to-image rules

Native generation works best with:

- one hero line of 4–10 Chinese characters;
- one support line of 8–18 characters;
- up to three short benefit titles;
- one short CTA for conversion, or one non-interactive slogan for publicity;
- one short badge.

For English native copy, use:

- one hero line of 1–8 words, preferably 2–6;
- one support line of 4–16 words;
- up to three benefit titles of 1–6 words each;
- one conversion CTA of 1–4 words, or no CTA for publicity;
- one badge of 1–4 words;
- one publicity slogan of 2–10 words when `requires_cta=false`.

Use exact-copy two-pass mode when the poster contains:

- prices or discount calculations;
- model numbers and specifications;
- legal or medical statements;
- more than roughly 45 Chinese characters or 35 English words total;
- dense service or platform terms.

When leaving space for later typesetting, explicitly describe the blank cards and their positions, but do not ask Seedream to draw random placeholder glyphs.

## 7. Richness without sameness

Build a lively poster with five controllable layers:

1. foreground frame: blur, cropped prop, tape, glass edge, arrow, or shadow;
2. hero layer: one large unobstructed product with a clean contour;
3. interaction layer: airflow, optical ray, splash, motion path, fabric, or usage result;
4. environment layer: pavilion, museum, street, home, arena, or narrative scene;
5. information layer: headline, benefits, badge, and CTA-or-slogan closure grouped into clear containers.

Use only layers that reinforce the buying idea. Do not repeat the same layer vocabulary across a batch.

Batch anti-template rule:

- only one poster may use the full “metallic H1 + three glass cards + circular portal + circular platform” stack;
- alternate high-key and low-key where product truth permits;
- alternate centered, asymmetric, diagonal, and collage compositions;
- alternate module shapes: glass cards, caption rules, chips, torn-paper tags, seals, or no containers;
- keep product truth and the intended CTA-or-slogan closure clear even when styles diverge.

## 8. Thumbnail and completion test

Before generation, imagine the poster at 180 px wide. It must still show:

- one dominant hook shape;
- one recognizable product silhouette;
- one distinct action/closing shape.

After generation, inspect the bottom 20%. Reject a poster that simply fades out. It needs one intentional close: CTA, offer bar, trust rail, or slogan with a framing line.
